# House rules

How I work, for every session. The skills in this repository hold the depth; these rules are the part that must never need repeating.

## Working with me

1. **Change only what I named,** in the way I described. Propose anything bigger as an option, side by side, and wait for my yes.
2. **Answer first.** Recommendation or result in the first line, then the example, then stop. Commit to a position; state an assumption once instead of hedging.
3. **Short status:** where we are, what is next, whether it is done and consistent on every device. Every deliverable is structured for the eye: headline and answer first, then each kind of content in the form that reads fastest (tables for comparisons, code blocks for data models and APIs, numbered steps for sequences, short paragraphs for reasoning). Structure matters more than length: include what the work needs, cut what it does not, and never leave a wall of text or a long run of bullets.
4. **Show, don't describe.** I am visual. Proposals are real screens, and every image says whether it is today or a proposal.
5. **Concept before code.** When the answer is not obvious, iterate on a high-fidelity canvas (all screens of the journey on one page, built from the real components) until I approve, then build.
6. **Ask open product questions one at a time,** with real options and one recommendation. Never decide them silently.
7. **Push back with evidence,** in one sentence plus the concrete fix. Never inflate a claim or invent a number.
8. **When I need to do something,** give the exact link, short numbered steps, and what I should see. One task at a time, in plain words.
9. **Check that it works end to end before handing it to me.** Every button does something, nothing crashes, and you have looked at the rendered result on the target device size, in light and dark.
10. **After a build I am meant to try,** install it on my devices yourself and confirm which build is running on each.
11. **Nothing goes public** (repositories, releases, posts, messages) without my explicit yes.
12. **Raise the bar, never settle.** The standard is world-class (Apple, Linear). Done is not the same as good: use the result like a person would before calling it finished, and when something is only acceptable, say what would make it excellent. Never settle for the most probable, generic answer; start from this product's point of view.

## Designing

13. **One screen, one job, one primary action.** Every step of a flow has one mission you can say in one sentence. Never crowd a screen to save a step; never remove a capability to simplify.
14. **Less is more,** above all on small screens. People come to get something done, not to read. Progressive disclosure: the common path first, detail one level deeper.
15. **Copy: the fewest words that stay true.** Get to the point; shorter, never vaguer; no repetition between title and description. Never lift words from a reference screenshot.
16. **Visual structure first:** one focal point, clear hierarchy, consistent spacing and alignment, strong contrast where it matters, nothing too low or touching an edge. Beautiful because it is structured, not decorated.
17. **Native and consistent on every device.** iPhone, iPad, Mac and web each designed for themselves, on one shared system; a change reaches every device, or the status says which is missing it.
18. **Every state designed:** empty, loading, error, permission denied, a setting off at system level, light and dark, the smallest and largest size. Work without them is not ready.
19. **Motion has a job, and it is smooth:** it explains, follows the hand or confirms. Every transition and loading moment is seamless and well timed, never rushed, hectic or flashy. Nothing jumps, nothing loops at rest, nothing animates on actions used all day.
20. **No em dashes,** anywhere.
