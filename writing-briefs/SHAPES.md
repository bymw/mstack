# Three shapes, and the words to swap

Read this at steps 3 and 8.

## A brief

For a reader who will decide, or forward it to someone who will.

```
[Title: the answer, as a statement]

We recommend [the one thing], because [the one reason that matters to this reader].

[The one visual]

What changes for [the person / the business / you]
  One short paragraph, or three bullets.

The options                  (only if there was a real choice)
  | Option | What it means | Cost or risk |   one marked Recommended

What we need from you
  The decision, the fact or the date, and by when.

To confirm
  Every figure or claim not yet confirmed, and who can confirm it.

Details                      (behind disclosure)
  Method, history, edge cases, links.
```

**It works when** the first two lines could be the whole message and the reader would still know what to do.

## A reply

For when someone asked a question and the user has to answer.

```
For you (not for sending):
  What they're really asking: [one line]

The reply:
  [The answer, first sentence.]
  [The reason or the one fact behind it.]
  [What happens next, or what we need from them.]
```

**It works when** it is short enough to read on a phone between meetings, and it answers what they asked rather than what we wanted to say.

## A deck outline

For a session. The outline is agreed in text before any slide is built, and the slides go inside the person's own template.

```
Session: [who, when, what they must leave with]

1. [Slide title that states the point]
   Visual: [the one picture]
   Say: [the one sentence the speaker must land]
2. …

Parked for the written follow-up:
  [Open questions, details, anything that would derail the session]
```

**It works when** every title read in order tells the whole story, and nothing on it recaps what the audience already knows.

## Decoding the ask

Before writing anything, answer these four in a line each:

1. **Who is asking, and what is their field?** A specialist's word weighs most in their own field.
2. **What did they literally ask?** Quote it.
3. **What do they need?** A decision, an answer, a document to forward, reassurance on a risk, or a reply they can act on.
4. **What would make them ask again?** That is the thing the first lines must settle.

## Swap the system word for the reader's

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| component, variant, kit | the button, the screen, the design system |
| token, CSS value | the colour, the size, the spacing |
| journey, flow | the steps the person takes to … |
| PR, merge, deploy | the change, ready for review; live |
| state, edge case | what the person sees when … |
| provider, API, integration | the service, the connection to … |
| spec, FR id | what we agreed the product does |
| persona, fixture | a person who … |
| regression | it broke again after it was fixed |
| refactor | tidied the code; nothing the person sees changes |
| "we leveraged / implemented / initiated" | we used / built / started |

Keep a term the reader uses themselves. If their field has a precise word for something, use their word, not a softer one.
