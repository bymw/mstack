# Axes: what makes two directions different

Read this at step 2, before making any direction. A direction is a position on an axis. Two directions that sit at the same position on every axis are one direction, however different their screenshots look.

## The axes that usually matter

**Grouping and structure.** What belongs with what, and what holds it together.
- Loose pills against one track of equal cells. A row of controls with one ground against two grounds.
- Ask: does the structure say which controls are one control?

**Ground and material.** What the content stands on, and how it is lit.
- Dark hero against light sheet, glass against solid, white card on grey against white on white.
- Ask: is every control legible on this ground, at 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for marks?

**Density and disclosure.** What shows at rest, and what waits behind a row, a sheet or a later step.
- Every figure on the hero against one figure with a line of context. A capped list against the whole list.
- Ask: what does the person need in the first three seconds?

**Position and reach.** Where a thing sits on the screen or window, and whether a thumb, pointer or keyboard gets there easily.
- Top against bottom, leading against trailing, pinned against scrolling with the content.
- Ask: is the primary action where the hand or eye already is, and away from the way out?

**Interaction model.** How the person acts on it.
- Tap against swipe against scrub. A sheet against a pushed page. Editing in place against a flow.
- Ask: is this the gesture the platform has taught them for this job?

**Signal.** How a state or a direction is carried.
- Colour against shape against glyph against words. Red and green arrows against symbols with colour confirming.
- Ask: can a colour-blind person tell the states apart?

**Motion.** What moves when the person acts, and how.
- A push against a rise against a crossfade in place.
- Directions on this axis are shown as recordings, never stills. Hold them to the project's motion vocabulary (`designing-motion`).

**Framing in words.** Only when the words are the idea, such as a figure stated as a total or as a change.
- Copy alternatives that say the same thing are tints, not directions.

## Tints to merge

These are not directions on their own:
- a different shade of the same colour
- a different radius, shadow strength or border weight
- synonyms in the copy
- a different icon for the same meaning, unless the icon is the question
- the same layout with a different spacing value (that is a `getting-sign-off` row, not a concept)

## Always consider

- **The best existing answer.** If another screen in the product already solves this job well, it is a direction. Reuse beats invention (`auditing-consistency`).
- **Today, kept.** It is a valid answer and stays on the canvas as the first panel.
- **The direction that removes the problem.** Sometimes the best answer is fewer controls, not a better-drawn control (`decluttering-screens`).

## How many

- **Two** when the question is binary: leading or trailing, sheet or page.
- **Three** by default.
- **Four** at most. A fifth dilutes the comparison. If the space really is wider, run a first round on the axis that matters most, then a second round inside the winner.
